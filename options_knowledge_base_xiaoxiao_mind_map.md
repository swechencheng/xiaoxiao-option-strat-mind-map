## 1. Market Environment Assessment (Pre-Trade Calibration)

### (1) Two Core Volatility Metrics
- **sd (Standard Deviation, Single-Day Expected Move)**: Priced via the **ATM straddle quote** for a given expiration (ATM Call price + ATM Put price); sd maps 1-to-1 with implied volatility ($\sigma$ dictates the normal distribution width mathematically). Longer DTE increases sd. It is NOT Bollinger Bands (which are lagging technical indicators, not market-clearing quotes).
- **VXN**: 30-day Nasdaq-100 Implied Volatility Index; reflects broader month-long volatility but does not fully represent single-day pricing (single-day expected moves require checking the 0DTE/1DTE ATM straddle).
- **IVR (IV Rank)**: When IVR < 20, buying 1–3 month long-dated calls "may be marginally favorable" (a rare, acceptable timing metric).
- **Key Terminology**: 1 sd move = value of the ATM straddle for that expiry; "a 3sd / 7sd decline" = an intraday drop equaling 3x or 7x the expected single-day straddle move.

### (2) Market Regimes & Optimal Strategy Matrix
1. **Severe Crash / Panic + High VXN (Market prices risk properly)**
   - Top choice: **Bearish Broken Wing Butterfly (BWB)** (0DTE or 1–3 weeks), net Delta 0.
   - May maintain a mild short delta bias via defined spreads (never via short underlying shares/futures).
2. **Elevated VXN / High Sustained Volatility (Following multi-day sell-offs)**
   - Extend expiration to **1–3 weeks** (under high IV, locking in longer-dated extrinsic value reduces tail exposure).
   - Execute a **Negative-Delta Iron Condor** (Call 0.5/0.2 + Put 0.12/0.05, net -0.2 delta).
3. **Suppressed VXN / Low Volatility Regime**
   - **Highest latent risk**: Buyers gamble on low probabilities while sellers "pick up pennies before steamrollers."
   - Extreme low IV renders **Bearish BWBs dangerous** (low IV forces the short put strikes too close to spot, vulnerable to quick breach).
   - Stance: **Sit out and wait**; strictly avoid extending DTE on the sell side.
4. **Range-Bound / Consolidation (Post-decline digestion or event vacuum)**
   - Execute a **Neutral Iron Condor** (Delta 0, profiting purely from time decay).
5. **Sharp Sell-Off Aftermath (Forced short-term bullishness, unable to sell further down)**
   - Execute an **Iron Condor** (1–2 weeks DTE); or sell deep OTM put spreads to acquire positive delta (0.3 / 0.1 strikes).
   - If IV remains elevated, lock in a 1–2 week Iron Condor: Call 0.5/0.35 + Put 0.25/0.10.
6. **Post-Parabolic Rally (Overbought conditions, short squeezes)**
   - Maintain a **bearish bias**; enter **Bearish Broken Wing Butterflies** or **Negative-Delta Iron Condors**.
7. **Expiration Week / Quadruple Witching / Quarterly Futures Roll**
   - Note that anticipated volatility is already priced into the ATM straddle; execute only defined-risk setups.
8. **Pre-Earnings / Earnings Announcement Days**
   - Treat as a 50/50 binary gamble; avoid predicting directional outcomes.
9. **Whipsaw / Choppy Regime (Frequent 3sd to 7sd outlier moves violating standard models)**
   - Core rule: **Defined risk only + extend DTE + avoid 0DTE**, giving positions wider breathing room.
   - Shift short put legs to **Deep OTM** (0.10–0.15 delta) for safety.
10. **Currencies / Long-Term Treasuries (Macro Niche)**
    - When bullish on JPY or US Long Bonds, sell naked puts (1 month DTE, 0.3–0.5 delta)—one of Xiaoxiao's rare exceptions permitting naked options (driven by low asset volatility and clear macro support floors).

## 2. Core Strategy Architecture & Leg Formations (Exhaustive)

### (1) Bearish Broken Wing Butterfly (BWB) — Signature Strategy
- **Leg Structure (2 Long, 2 Short = 4 Legs, Net Delta 0, Debit Entry)**:
  - Buy 1 **Delta 0.5 Put** (ATM)
  - Sell 2 **Delta 0.3 Puts** (or Delta 0.4)
  - Buy 1 **Delta 0.1 Put** (or 0.05, deepest OTM, forming the "broken wing" that caps downside risk)
- **Standard Benchmark Configuration**: **0.50 / 0.40 / 0.15 / 0.05** (preferred by Xiaoxiao for consistency and stability).
- **Acceptable Alternatives**: 0.5/0.3/0.3/0.1, or 0.5/0.35/0.2/0.05—**the sole requirement is that total net delta equals 0**.
- **Expiration Selection**: 0DTE or 1–3 weeks. **Only 0DTE generates substantial immediate profitability** (magnifying the time decay divergence across the 4 strikes); in longer-dated BWBs, the 4 legs offset each other, leaving Delta-0 net value largely static (e.g., a 45-day structure gained only $1 after 14 days).
- **Profit Mechanism**: A Theta-decay engine with neutral Vega balance (2 long vs. 2 short insulate against IV shifts). Profit peaks when the underlying declines towards the short strike, exploiting the decay gap between strikes.
- **Empirical 0DTE P&L Case Study (Entry Debit: ~$0.92 pts)**:
  - Underlying drops 9 pts to the short strikes after 24 hours: Structure value expands to 5.49 pts (~6x gain).
  - Underlying remains flat: Structure value expands to 1.99 pts (~2x gain via theta decay).
  - Underlying rallies 5 pts: Structure value drops to 0.48 pts (~50% loss, manageable or scratchable with early exit).
- **Maximum Loss** = Difference between wing widths (e.g., upper width 9 pts vs. lower broken wing width 15 pts = 6 pts net width). On QQQ, margin requirement = $6 \times 100 = \$600$; on NQ futures (8x QQQ), max loss = $6 \times 20 \times 40 = \$4,800$.
- In live execution, cutting the trade if price reaches the deepest long put results in an actual loss of ~$4–5 pts (initial debit $0.92 + remaining time value loss on the lowest put).
- **Wing Width Implications**: In standard setups (e.g., 200 pt upper vs. 800 pt lower width), a catastrophic market circuit breaker could inflict $(800 - 200) \times 20 = \$12,000$ per contract.
- **Why Broken Wing over Classic Butterfly**: A classic butterfly (symmetric wings) requires shifting the short puts lower, turning delta negative and reducing entry credit; this flips the position to negative theta (paying decay) and imposes directional vulnerability from day one.

### (2) Iron Condor (IC)
- **Leg Structure (2 Short, 2 Long = 4 Legs, Credit Entry)**:
  - Sell 1 Call Spread (Short delta 0.50, Long delta 0.40) to short the upside
  - Sell 1 Put Spread (Short delta 0.15, Long delta 0.05) to monetize the downside
- **Core Concept**: Net seller strategy capturing time decay with capped risk, suited for volatile markets.
- **Strike Selection**: On equity indices, prioritize **selling Call Spreads containing ATM calls** (controlling upside risk) + **selling Put Spreads in OTM puts (0.10–0.30 delta)** (exploiting put skew).
- **Directional Variants by Net Delta**:
  - **Neutral Iron Condor**: Call 0.5/0.3 + Put 0.3/0.1.
  - **Bearish Iron Condor**: Call 0.5/0.4 + Put 0.15/0.05.
  - **Negative-Delta Iron Condor** (Xiaoxiao's personal setup): Call 0.5/0.2 + Put 0.12/0.05 $\rightarrow$ Net -0.2 Delta.
  - **Positive-Delta Iron Condor** (Dip-buying): Call 0.5/0.4 + Put 0.3/0.1.
  - 1–2 Week Variant: Call 0.5/0.35 + Put 0.25/0.10.
- **Put-Call Parity Equivalence with Bearish BWB**:
  - Bearish BWB = Buy upper Put Spread (0.5/0.4) + Sell lower Put Spread as a hedge.
  - Iron Condor = Sell upper Call Spread (0.5/0.4) + Sell lower Put Spread as a hedge.
  - **Why two distinct names**: **Liquidity management**. To avoid trading illiquid deep ITM options (delta > 0.70). If attempting to sell an upper 0.5/0.4 Call Spread after a drop turns it into an ITM 0.6/0.5 spread, switch to buying an ATM 0.5/0.4 Put Spread (the BWB).
- **Iron Condor vs. BWB Profit Profiles**: Iron Condors yield higher profits during flat consolidation (since the short call is ATM); BWBs achieve maximum profit only after price declines to the short put strike. The downside of the Iron Condor is that during a severe plunge, put spread losses can easily outstrip call spread gains.

### (3) Deep OTM Put Credit Spread (Dip-Buying Mechanism)
- **Leg Structure (1 Short, 1 Long = 2 Legs)**:
  - Sell 1 **Delta 0.15 Put** (or 0.13 / 0.10, deep OTM)
  - Buy 1 **Delta 0.05 Put** (or 0.09 / 0.05)
- **Net Delta per Tranche**: **+0.04 to +0.05** (higher delta = more aggressive; 0.10 delta doubles the risk/reward).
- **Expiration Target**: **2–5 Days** (recommended: 5 days / Friday expiry); when VXN spikes, extend to 1–2 weeks; when VXN is low, never sell puts beyond 2 weeks.
- **Dip-Buying Thesis**: Following a sharp drop, elevated IV combined with index put skew inflates OTM puts, creating positive expectancy for put credit spreads. Even if the underlying continues to drift downward, the position remains fully profitable as long as strikes are not breached.
- **Scaling In**: Add one tranche (+0.04 to +0.05 delta) for every 100–200 point drop, assigning each tranche a distinct expiration date to diversify single-day gap risk.
- **Never use this structure to "take assignment"**: Xiaoxiao explicitly rejects selling puts to acquire shares; selling puts to take delivery is a disguised, inefficient long bet that exposes the trader to severe downside assignment risk.

### (4) Covered Call / Cash-Secured Put (Proper Bullish Positioning)
- **If holding cash**: Buy 100 shares + Sell 1-month ATM Call (strike $\approx$ spot price).
- **If cash-constrained**: Sell 1–2 month ATM naked Put.
- **Put-Call Parity Rule**: Long 100 shares + Short Call (same strike/expiry) $\equiv$ Short Put.
- If you are inclined to sell a 95-strike OTM naked put, you should be equally comfortable buying 100 shares and selling a 95-strike ITM call; otherwise, your thesis is mathematically inconsistent.
- Hedging long stock: Selling a 1-month ATM call adds -0.50 delta (equivalent to cutting 50% of your equity position). Treat this as a structural risk-reduction hedge, not mere "income generation."

### (5) Classic Butterfly (Symmetric Wings — Counterexample / Tactical Tool)
- Equal wing widths prevent achieving Delta 0 without introducing directional bias and negative theta; Xiaoxiao does not endorse this as an initial trade.
- Utilized exclusively as an emergency adjustment tool to rescue breached short puts (see Defense section).

### (6) Put Ratio Spread (1:2 — Counterexample / Prohibited)
- Buying 1 delta 0.50 put and selling 2 delta 0.25 puts leaves the lower wing naked with uncapped downside risk.
- While it collects credits 90% of the time, an extreme 7sd crash inflicts a catastrophic $25 loss compared to just $4–5 on a BWB.

### (7) Naked Put Selling (Treasuries / JPY Exception)
- Long **30-Year Treasury Futures (ZB)** or **Japanese Yen Futures (JPY)**: Sell naked puts, ~1 month DTE, 0.30–0.50 delta.
- Allowed solely on macro underlyings exhibiting low beta, clear fundamental support levels, and small contract values.

### (8) Diagonal Spreads / Calendar Spreads (Caution / Counterexample)
- Spreading across disparate expirations introduces massive IV mispricing risk.
- Buying far-dated options in high IV is fatal (subsequent IV crush collapses far-dated extrinsic value faster than near-dated decay).
- Diagonals are marginally viable only in low-IV regimes (buying cheap back-month extrinsic while selling near-month).

### (9) LEAPS Calls / Poor Man's Covered Calls (Counterexample)
- Opposed to buying LEAPS calls to express bullishness: paying peak IV for back-month extrinsic value means future equity gains will be erased by IV normalization.
- To acquire leveraged long exposure, buy NQ/MNQ futures directly rather than long calls, eliminating IV drag entirely.

## 3. Expiration Selection Logic (Exhaustive)

- **0DTE / Same-Day Expirations**: Only the **Bearish Broken Wing Butterfly** functions effectively here (widening the time decay divergence across legs). 0DTE Iron Condors exhibit hyper-accelerated theta decay but subject the trader to extreme negative gamma risk, drastically increasing operational errors.
- **Same-Day vs. Next-Day Entry**: If entering during the morning session, select same-day expiration; if entering in the afternoon or evening session, select next-day expiration.
- **1–3 Week Horizons**: When markets ignore standard deviation bands (whipsaw regimes), extend DTE to 1–3 weeks with defined-risk spreads to absorb wide price swings and preserve adjustment flexibility.
- **High VXN $\rightarrow$ Extend DTE**; **Low VXN $\rightarrow$ Stick to Short-Term / 0DTE** (selling far-dated options in low IV creates disastrous downside vulnerability).
- **Prioritize Friday Expirations** (optimal market liquidity); avoid trading newly listed off-cycle daily cycles lacking established market-maker pricing and liquidity.
- **Avoid Ultra-Long DTE (>90 Days)**: Severe liquidity shortages produce punitive bid-ask spreads.
- **When to Extend DTE**: During steep sell-offs as VXN climbs, locking in elevated extrinsic premium to protect against further downside.
- **When to Shorten / Avoid Extending DTE**: During market rallies as IV deflates; never sell long-dated extrinsic value at IV bottoms.
- **Quarterly Futures Expiration Nuances (4 times per year, 3rd Friday of end-of-quarter month)**:
  - September NQ futures and options settle at **9:30 AM open via cash settlement**; OTM options expire worthless, while ITM options are assigned and settled against the NDX opening quote.
  - A parallel option chain referencing the December NQ contract expires at the **4:00 PM market close**.
  - Futures expiration day options offer peak volume and tighter spreads; however, by Thursday afternoon, volume on the expiring contract evaporates as institutional liquidity rolls forward into December.

## 5. Position Monitoring & Active Risk Tracking

1. **Net Aggregate Portfolio Delta** (SPX beta-weighted delta or summed leg delta):
   - **Exceeding $\pm 0.3$ warrants active vigilance; exceeding $\pm 0.5$ triggers high alert** (indicates severe directional bias, transforming the trade into a directional gamble).
2. **Single-Day Expected Move (sd)** (Current 0DTE ATM straddle price):
   - Track to evaluate whether intraday swings threaten structural thresholds.
3. **VXN Index Levels**:
   - Informs decisions on extending or contracting DTE, and whether net selling remains mathematically viable.
4. **Primary Short Strike Breach**:
   - A price break through the first short strike signals immediate risk elevation.
5. **Leg Delta Rebalancing Thresholds**:
   - Upper Short Call delta reaching 0.35 (falling OTM) $\rightarrow$ initiate downward roll.
   - Lower Long Put delta reaching 0.35, or Upper Long Call delta reaching 0.65 $\rightarrow$ execute defense.
6. **Theta Decay Tracking**:
   - Theta decays continuously tick-by-tick across the spread. Monitor the 0DTE ATM straddle price to gauge market tension and decay velocity.
7. **Dynamic Breakeven Migration**:
   - Breakeven points shift dynamically with each roll; evaluate trades based on **forward mathematical expectancy (+EV)** rather than historic cost basis.
8. **Explicit "No Action Required" Scenarios**:
   - Portfolio net delta remains near 0 and expiration is over a week away $\rightarrow$ do not adjust.
   - Price rallies against a Bearish BWB with more than 3 days to expiry $\rightarrow$ no adjustment needed (BWB carries zero upside risk).

## 6. Adjustment Strategies & Defensive Playbooks (Critical Core)

### (1) Core Defensive Philosophy
- **Only Three Legitimate Adjustments**: Let options expire worthless, scale into new tranches, or roll out in time. **There is no concept of "panic liquidating at market"** (unless permanently retiring from trading).
- **Adjustments inherently carry negative expectancy** (~$20 in transaction drag per action). The sole justification for adjusting is preventing a catastrophic maximum loss.
- **Minimize Roll Frequency**: Rolling is warranted only to salvage a position that would otherwise realize full loss; rolling a healthy position will not increase net returns.
- **Paying a debit to roll does NOT equal losing money; collecting a credit does NOT equal making money**: Paying a debit buys back higher-value options, raising future expected payoff.
- **Reject Mechanical Stop-Losses and Profit Targets**: Stop-losses at the lows force liquidation at peak IV; profit-taking early surrenders free tail premium.
- **"Don't worry about where it came from, only worry about where it's going"**: Decisions must be grounded entirely on the forward mathematical expectancy of the resulting structure.

### (2) Quantitative Adjustment Triggers
- Upper **Short Call (OTM) drops 200 points** below market spot $\rightarrow$ initiate a roll downward and outward (200 pts is a strict rule).
- Spot breaches the first short strike by **>100 points** $\rightarrow$ evaluate defense; breaches by **>300 points** $\rightarrow$ execute roll immediately.
- Upper **Short Call delta drops to 0.40 $\rightarrow$ prepare roll; drops to 0.30 $\rightarrow$ mandatory roll** (standard baseline: 0.35 delta).
- Lower **Short Put reaches 0.65 delta / Long Put reaches 0.35 delta / Long Call reaches 0.65 delta** $\rightarrow$ execute rebalancing.
- **3 Days Prior to Expiration**: Mandatory portfolio review regardless of strike location (assess whether to roll or hold to settlement).

### (3) Downside Defense: Rolling Upper Bearish Spreads
- **Action**: Close the legacy upper spread $\rightarrow$ sell an **ATM Call Spread for the following week (Sell ATM Call, Buy OTM Call)**, configuring the new 4-leg structure to **Delta 0**.
- **Always extend expiration by +1 week per roll** (during sell-offs, surging VXN allows locking in higher extrinsic value while hedging downside risk).
- **Rolling outward dampens negative gamma**, reducing the need for subsequent frequent rolls as the structure stabilizes.
- **Rolling every 200 pts vs. rolling every 400 pts**: Final P&L divergence is minimal; rolling less frequently saves on transaction slippage, but in a steep waterfall decline, an excessively OTM call spread fails to capture sufficient downside delta to offset put losses.
- **Why rolling down is beneficial**: Index put skew makes selling deep OTM calls negative EV; roll OTM calls down to ATM promptly to monetize extrinsic decay.

### (4) Severe Downside Defense: Rescuing Breached Short Put Spreads (The 6-Leg Roll)
- **Deploy a Classic Butterfly to Roll Downward (6-Leg Procedure)**:
  1. Buy back the existing, breached short put.
  2. Sell 2 lower-strike puts (targeting delta 0.25–0.30).
  3. Buy 1 lowest-strike put.
  - *Result*: Establishes a same-expiry **Classic Symmetric Butterfly**, sized to 1 sd in width (~200 points wide).
- **A classic butterfly introduces net negative delta** $\rightarrow$ balance by **selling a deep OTM put credit spread expiring the following week**, restoring the total 6-leg structure to **Net Delta 0**.
- **Advantages of the Classic Butterfly Defense**:
  1. Identical expiration eliminates "extra day" timing risk.
  2. Equal buy/sell contracts neutralize Vega and Gamma exposure.
  3. Minimal capital outlay without ballooning margin.
  4. Allows time decay to run until the short put decays to zero, avoiding realized losses.
- **Splitting Breached 0DTE Delta**: Decompose an unmanageable 0.60 delta breach into 0.20–0.30 on the current day + 0.30–0.40 on Monday, diffusing risk over multiple sessions.
- **If long ATM calls stall during flat chop**: Roll upward ~100 points and purchase a next-day ATM call spread to maintain Delta 0 (managing Vega/Theta decay).
- **Why use short horizons (2–5 days) to defend puts rather than far months**: Short-dated gamma expires quickly, establishing certainty; far-dated options double the timing risk. Novices should target 5 days (Friday).
- **Never roll an entire breached put spread out to far months in one block**: This crystalizes catastrophic losses and leaves the account trapped in a downward spiral praying for a bounce.
- **Catastrophic Tail Events**: The best move during an uncontainable crash is to **accept the predefined maximum loss**; over-adjusting into a steamroller amplifies destruction.

### (5) Upside Defense: Managing Bullish Squeezes
- **Bearish Broken Wing Butterflies have NO upside risk**: Maximum loss is strictly capped at the initial entry debit; during strong rallies, structure losses remain tiny. If price rallies with >3 days to expiration, **no adjustment is required even if upper strikes are passed**.
- **Managing Deep ITM Short Call Spreads**: When the long call delta reaches ~0.65 $\rightarrow$ roll into a Bearish BWB:
  - Close the short call spread $\rightarrow$ buy an **ATM Put Spread (Buy 0.50 Put, Sell 0.40 Put)**, targeting Delta 0 **in the same expiration (do not extend DTE, as rallies compress IV)**.
- Apply Put-Call Parity: Avoid holding deep ITM short puts (which are mirrors of OTM short calls).
- **[The Fundamental Edge of Defined Spreads (Sept 18 Deep Dive)]**:
  - Why short via spreads rather than shorting underlying shares or futures?
  - When a massive rally overshoots the spread, the long leg moves deep ITM, retaining substantial extrinsic value. Liquidating the long leg locks in this residual extrinsic premium, partially offsetting short losses (losing far less than naked shorts).
  - You can then buy an ATM put spread at the higher price level: the captured extrinsic credit finances a reset at superior levels, enabling the trader to reach breakeven without requiring price to drop all the way back to original entry—simply dropping to the *new* short put strike recovers full equity.
  - *Prerequisite*: **Maintain thesis consistency**. If you chose to short at the bottom, you must short with greater conviction at higher, more favorable prices; shorting the low and panic-buying the top is textbook retail destruction.

### (6) Dip-Buying with Positive Delta (Post-Crash Strategy)
- After severe sell-offs, elevated IV combined with put skew makes selling deep OTM put spreads statistically positive EV, aligning with a broader bearish thesis.
- **Method A**: Adjust the upper roll structure to target a slight positive net delta (+0.04 to +0.05) instead of pure zero.
- **Method B**: Directly sell a standalone deep OTM put credit spread (delta 0.04–0.05, 2–5 days DTE).
- **Position Sizing**: Allocate +0.04 to +0.05 delta per tranche (+0.07 to +0.10 for aggressive dip-buying).
- **Add tranches every 100–200 point decline across staggered expirations**; if net positive delta becomes excessive, halt new put sales and roll upper call spreads at Delta 0.

### (7) Delta Portfolio Management
- **Avoid non-zero delta adjustments under normal conditions**; accept natural delta fluctuations driven by price action (just as long call holders accept fluctuating delta).
- The objective is ensuring the **4 legs of the active adjustment roll equal Delta 0**, not forcing the entire historical account to zero.
- Delta displayed on IBKR NQ screens is multiplied by 20; divide by 20 (or multiply by 0.05) to compute true contract delta.
- Small decimals (0.00x) can be ignored; however, 0.099 $\approx$ 0.10 is significant and should be trimmed by shifting strikes.
- If portfolio delta drifts too negative at market bottoms, sell deep OTM put spreads to replenish positive delta rather than chasing price higher.

### (8) Classic Operational Mistakes (Repeatedly corrected by Xiaoxiao)
- **Panic Selling at Market Bottoms**: Liquidating long positions or cutting losses at troughs combines bad market timing with peak IV crush, vaporizing accounts rapidly.
- **Chasing Rallies by Flipping Bullish**: Panic-closing short calls at the high to sell puts guarantees maximum loss when the market reverses.
- **Rigid 80% Profit / 50% Stop-Loss Rules**: Arbitrary rules without mathematical basis surrender the final 20% in risk-free decay to market makers.
- **Unbundling Tight Spreads**: Spreads with narrow widths (e.g., 29200/29100) have near-zero delta; breaking them apart leaves isolated directional risk.
- **Oversized Dip-Buying (+0.09 Delta in a single tranche)**: Exceeds the +0.04–0.05 risk threshold and concentrates exposure in a single expiration.
- **Technical Roll Failures (Sept 18 Lessons)**: Selecting an expiration that is too near-dated, choosing non-integer strikes, rolling during illiquid pre-market hours, or executing non-neutral rolls (e.g., net -0.60 delta)—these mistakes ruin structural defense.
- **Allowing a Free Roll to Degenerate into a "Horse Race"**: Failing to manage short calls with 3 days remaining and dragging them into Quadruple Witching cash settlement leaves the trade entirely at the mercy of Friday's 9:30 AM open ("a free roll degraded into a horse race").

## 7. Order Execution & Tactical Routing Details (Exhaustive)

- **Constructing Delta-0 Orders**:
  1. Post a limit order at the **bid price** to force market makers to tighten the quote.
  2. Once delta stabilizes, modify the limit price to **midpoint minus $0.25**.
  3. Walk the order up incrementally by **+$0.25** until filled. If the fill results in a non-zero delta, cancel and adjust one strike.
- **Wide Bid-Ask Spreads (> $5.00)**:
  - Submit at the bid to inspect real-time response $\rightarrow$ adjust to mid - $0.25 \rightarrow$ step up incrementally.
- **Closing Positions**:
  - Place a limit order at midpoint + $0.02$, stepping down by -$0.01$ until filled; on illiquid underlyings, begin at midpoint + $0.30$.

  - Check ATM bid-ask spread width: $2–$3 is optimal, $4 is fair, $6 is poor. Avoid underlyings whose options do not quote down to two decimal places.

- **Expiration Diversification**:
  - Selling multiple tranches of 0DTE put spreads creates unmanaged leverage; a single tail event can blow up an account. Always disperse positions across multiple expirations.

## 8. Option Greeks & Pricing Principles (Exhaustive)

- **Mapping sd to Implied Volatility**: sd equals the current ATM straddle quote. Implied volatility ($\sigma$) determines distribution width mathematically. As DTE expands, sd increases; however, future IV trajectory remains unpredictable.
- **The Irrelevance of Vega**: A classic chicken-and-egg dilemma—does declining IV lower prices, or does lower realized volatility compress IV? Because IV shifts cannot be forecasted reliably, ignore Vega and focus on Theta and Gamma.
- **The Inextricable Theta-Gamma Trade-Off**: Positive theta mandates negative gamma; negative gamma creates an "inverted bowl" payoff curve that suffers under outsized moves in either direction.
- **Charm Dynamics ($d\Delta/dt$)**: Short OTM put spreads carry negative charm; as expiration approaches and the options decay toward zero, delta decays to zero—which functions as a synthetic short hedge against the downside.
- **Option Value Surface $V(S, t, \sigma)$**: Option pricing represents a 4-dimensional manifold. Broker P&L diagrams are merely 2-dimensional cross-sections holding $\sigma$ and $t$ static.
- **Interpreting P&L Graphs**: Horizontal axis = Spot Price, Vertical axis = P&L. Green line = Current P&L; Blue line = Future P&L at target date. The blue curve shifts upward over time via theta decay while maintaining its negative gamma "bowl" curvature. These diagrams represent forward-looking expectations; past P&L cannot be altered.
- **The Asymmetry of Deep OTM Options**: 99% probability of earning 1% vs. 1% probability of losing 10–20%; Xiaoxiao strictly rejects trading deep OTM naked options.
- **Put Skew vs. Call Skew Mechanics**:
  - Equity index options feature persistent **put skew** (OTM puts trade at high implied volatility due to downside crash hedging demand).
  - Speculative growth stocks feature persistent **call skew** (retail and fund demand for calls outstrips supply, as covered call sellers require 100 shares per contract while naked call sellers are scarce).
- **Put-Call Parity Foundation**:
  - Long Put Spread $\equiv$ Short Call Spread.
  - Long 100 shares + Short Call $\equiv$ Short Put.
  - This mathematical parity underpins all conversions between Broken Wing Butterflies and Iron Condors.
- **Market Makers as Algorithmic Arbitrageurs**: ~80% of underlying equity volume originates from option dealer hedging ("the tail wags the dog"). In orderly markets, retail option buyers lose money; during extreme tail events, market makers take heavy losses through automated delta-hedging stops.
- **The Zero-Sum Reality**: Traders running negative mathematical expectancy (e.g., constant market-timing, panic-selling at lows) fund the profits of disciplined structural traders.
- **Demystifying GEX, Gamma Squeezes & Option Walls**: Net Gamma Exposure (GEX) yields no alpha (dealers and institutions already price it in). The retail "gamma squeeze" narrative is largely a myth—rally momentum is propelled by institutional and retail capital buying calls aggressively, not dealer short-covering alone.
- **Technical Indicators vs. Market Quotes**: Technical indicators like Bollinger Bands lack empirical pricing authority; market-clearing quotes from ATM straddles (sd) provide the only true measure of market-implied variance.

## 9. Accounting & Tax Bookkeeping

- **Maintain Two Distinct Accounting Ledgers**:
  - Ledger A: Net Cash Premium (Total debits paid vs. credits collected + current Net Market Value to liquidate).
  - Ledger B: Closed Realized P&L vs. Open Unrealized P&L.

- **Bookkeeping Multiple Rolls**:
  - Rolling down locks in realized profits on the initial spread; when the lower puts decay to zero, additional profit is realized.
  - Concurrently, unrealized losses on deeply ITM short call spreads accumulate during rallies until the position is rolled upward into an ATM long put spread, at which point the losses are realized and offset.
