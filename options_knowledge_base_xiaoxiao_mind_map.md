## 1. Market Environment Assessment (Pre-Trade Calibration)

### (1) Core Volatility Metrics
- **sd (Standard Deviation, Single-Day Expected Move)**: Priced via the **ATM straddle quote** for a given expiration (ATM Call price + ATM Put price, Cases 2, 4, 6, 19, 28, 46); sd maps 1-to-1 with implied volatility ($\sigma$ dictates the normal distribution width mathematically). Longer DTE increases sd. It is NOT Bollinger Bands (which are lagging technical indicators, not market quotes).
  - **Overnight sd vs. Full-Day sd (Cases 28–30)**: AM-settled options expiring at 9:30 AM (e.g., Quadruple Witching) price only the **overnight sd** to the 9:30 AM open (~100 pts on NQ), whereas 4:00 PM PM-settled options price the **full-day sd**.
- **VXN**: 30-day Nasdaq-100 Implied Volatility Index; reflects broader month-long volatility but does not fully represent single-day pricing (single-day expected moves require checking the 0DTE/1DTE ATM straddle).
- **IVR (IV Rank)**: When IVR < 20, buying 1–3 month long-dated calls "may be marginally favorable."
- **Key Terminology**: 1 sd move = value of the ATM straddle for that expiry; "a 3sd / 7sd decline" = an intraday drop equaling 3x or 7x the expected single-day straddle move (under a normal distribution, $>3\text{sd}$ has $<1\%$ probability, yet occurs frequently during illiquid night/holiday sessions due to dealer Dynamic Delta Hedging, Cases 80, 89, 93).

### (2) Market Regimes & Optimal Strategy Matrix
1. **Severe Crash / Panic + High VXN (Market prices risk properly)**
   - Top choice: **Bearish Broken Wing Butterfly (BWB) (0DTE/1DTE or 1–3 weeks)**, net Delta 0 (Cases 1, 68, 72).
   - Two-step **"Legging In / Sell Into Strength" entry (Case 69)**: When expecting a drop but fearing downside risk, open only the upper ATM Call Spread (`0.5/0.2`) at the 4:00 PM close; once a night-session drop + IV spike hits, roll the call spread down and sell an ultra-wide deep OTM put spread to complete the Delta-0 Iron Condor.
2. **Elevated VXN / Whipsaw Regime (Multi-day sell-offs or frequent 3sd swings, Cases 68–119)**
   - Extend expiration to **1–3 weeks (8–20 DTE)** (under high IV, locking in longer-dated extrinsic value reduces tail exposure and provides wide downside breathing room).
   - Primary recommendation: **1–3 week Bearish BWB (`0.5 / 0.4 / 0.15 / 0.05`)** or **1–3 week Bearish Iron Condor**, earning substantial positive daily Theta (`+$70 to +$178/day` on NQ, Cases 73, 83, 113).
3. **Suppressed VXN / Very Low Single-Day sd**
   - **Highest latent risk**: Buyers gamble on low probabilities while sellers "pick up pennies before steamrollers."
   - **When single-day sd is very low, 0DTE Bearish BWBs are NOT recommended** (Case 242: low IV places the short put strikes too close to spot, making them easy to breach); either **sit out and wait**, trade a **0DTE/1DTE Iron Condor** if actively monitoring the screen (Cases 6, 49, 63), or use a **1–3 week `0.5/0.4/0.15/0.05` BWB** (Case 232). Strictly avoid being a far-dated net seller in low IV.
4. **Range-Bound / Consolidation (Post-decline digestion or event vacuum)**
   - Execute a **Neutral Iron Condor** (Delta 0, profiting purely from time decay).
5. **Sharp Sell-Off Aftermath (Forced short-term bullishness, unable to sell further down)**
   - Execute an **Iron Condor** (1–2 weeks DTE, e.g., `Call 0.5/0.35 + Put 0.25/0.10`); or sell deep OTM put spreads to acquire positive delta (`0.15/0.05` or `0.30/0.10`).
6. **Post-Parabolic Rally (Overbought conditions, short squeezes)**
   - Maintain a **bearish bias**; enter **Bearish Broken Wing Butterflies** or **Bearish Iron Condors**.
7. **Expiration Week / Quadruple Witching / Quarterly Futures Roll**
   - Anticipated volatility is priced into the ATM straddle; stick to defined-risk strategies, typically opening for next week's Friday expiration.
8. **Pre-Earnings / Earnings Announcement Days**
   - Treat as a 50/50 binary gamble; avoid predicting directional outcomes.
9. **Currencies / Long-Term Treasuries (Macro Niche)**
   - When bullish on JPY or US Long Bonds, sell naked puts (1 month DTE, 0.30–0.50 delta)—one of Xiaoxiao's rare exceptions permitting naked options (driven by low asset volatility and clear macro support floors).

## 2. Core Strategy Architecture & Leg Formations (Exhaustive)

### (1) Bearish Broken Wing Butterfly (BWB) — Signature Strategy
- **Two Specific Leg Structures (2 Long, 2 Short = 4 Legs, Net Delta 0, Small Debit Entry, Cases 1, 68, 72, 160)**:
  1. **3-Strike Classic BWB (`1:2:1` structure, used for 0DTE in early June, Case 1)**:
     - Buy 1 **Delta 0.50 Put** (ATM)
     - Sell 2 **Delta 0.30 Puts** (at the same middle strike)
     - Buy 1 **Delta 0.10 Put** (deepest OTM, the "broken wing" capping downside risk)
  2. **4-Strike Split BWB (`1:1:1:1` structure — Xiaoxiao's most frequent standard benchmark: `0.50 / 0.40 / 0.15 / 0.05`, preferred "for stability", Cases 68–119, 160, 232)**:
     - Buy 1 Upper Put Debit Spread: **Buy 1 Delta 0.50 Put + Sell 1 Delta 0.40 Put** (bearish leg)
     - Sell 1 Lower Deep OTM Put Credit Spread: **Sell 1 Delta 0.15 Put + Buy 1 Delta 0.05 Put** (hedges net Delta to 0)
     - Acceptable alternatives: `0.5/0.35/0.2/0.05`, etc.—**the core rules are that total net Delta equals 0, and the lower spread width MUST be at least 2x the upper spread width** (Case 196: e.g., upper width 200–300 pts vs. lower width 600–1000 pts).
- **Why a Delta-0 Bearish BWB Must Enter for a Debit & Barely Loses on Rallies (Cases 72, 73, 113, 114)**:
  - A Delta-0 Bearish BWB must enter for a small debit (entering for a credit would give it positive delta and much heavier downside risk, Case 72).
  - That entry debit (e.g., ~$1,100 on NQ for a 2-week BWB) is NOT lost on entry: closing after a few days recovers nearly the full principal plus positive Theta (`~$178/day`), and even in a strong rally you recover most of it because the lower sold Put Spread is much wider than the upper bought Put Spread and benefits from index **Put Skew**, offsetting the upper spread's loss (Cases 73, 113, 114). Conversely, a **Bullish Call BWB is unviable on equity indices** because put skew makes upper OTM calls too cheap to provide protection (Cases 7, 114).
- **Expiration Selection (0DTE vs. 1–3 Weeks, Cases 1, 67, 68–119, 232, 242)**:
  - **0DTE / 1DTE**: When VXN / single-day sd is elevated, a 0DTE BWB maximizes time-decay differentials across strikes without the extreme upside negative-gamma danger of a 0DTE ATM short call (Case 67); enter 0DTE in the morning, or 1DTE in the afternoon/close.
  - **1–3 Weeks (8–28 DTE)**: Across dozens of daily reports in late June–August, Xiaoxiao recommended **1–3 week `0.5/0.4/0.15/0.05` BWBs** (Cases 68–119, 227, 232), generating `+$50 to +$178/day` in positive Theta (Case 73) and held for at least 3+ days (Case 75); however, avoid 45-DTE horizons where wing decay cancels out ($1 gain over 14 days).
- **Empirical 0DTE P&L Case Study (Entry Debit: ~$0.92 pts, Case 1)**:
  - Underlying drops 9 pts to the short strikes after 24 hours: Structure value expands to 5.49 pts (~6x gain).
  - Underlying remains flat: Structure value expands to 1.99 pts (~2x gain via theta decay).
  - Underlying rallies 5 pts: Structure value drops to 0.48 pts (~50% loss, manageable with early exit).
- **Maximum Loss** = Difference between wing widths (e.g., 200 pt upper vs. 800 pt lower width = $(800 - 200) \times 20 = \$12,000$ in a circuit-breaker crash; on QQQ, 9 pt vs. 15 pt width = 6 pt gap = $600, or $4,800 on NQ). Cutting a 0DTE trade early if spot reaches the lowest strike salvages $2–3 of residual extrinsic value on the lowest put, limiting actual loss to ~$4–5 pts (Case 2).
- **Why Broken Wing over Classic Butterfly**: A classic butterfly (symmetric wings) shifts the 2 short puts lower, turning net delta negative and flipping the structure to negative theta (paying time decay).

### (2) Iron Condor (IC)
- **Leg Structure (2 Short, 2 Long = 4 Legs, Credit Entry)**:
  - Sell 1 Call Spread (Short delta 0.50, Long delta 0.40) to short the upside
  - Sell 1 Put Spread (Short delta 0.15, Long delta 0.05) to monetize the downside
- **Core Concept & Expiration Selection (Cases 6, 28, 49, 63, 143, 160)**: Net seller strategy capturing time decay with capped risk. **In high IV select longer expirations (1–3 weeks); in low IV (or when actively monitoring the screen) 0DTE/1DTE Iron Condors (`末日铁鹰`) are viable** (Case 63).
- **Strike Selection & Wing-Width Ratio Rule**: On equity indices, prioritize **selling Call Spreads containing ATM calls** (controlling upside risk) + **selling Put Spreads in OTM puts (0.10–0.30 delta)** (exploiting put skew); **the lower Put Spread width must be at least 2x the upper Call Spread width** (Case 196).
- **Directional Variants by Net Delta**:
  - **Neutral Iron Condor**: Call 0.5/0.3 + Put 0.3/0.1.
  - **Bearish Iron Condor (Standard Benchmark)**: Call 0.5/0.4 + Put 0.15/0.05 (Net Delta 0).
  - **1–2 Week Variant**: Call 0.5/0.35 + Put 0.25/0.10.
  - **Positive-Delta Iron Condor** (Dip-buying): Call 0.5/0.4 + Put 0.3/0.1.
  - **Important Clarification on "Negative-Delta (-0.2) Iron Condor" (Cases 69, 145, 160)**: In Case 160, Xiaoxiao strictly warned **against entering a new trade at `-0.20 delta` to gamble on direction** (equivalent to shorting $120k / 170 shares of QQQ naked); his own `-0.20 delta` positions came either from two-step legging-in across a crash (Case 69) or from legacy deep-ITM call spreads acting as static delta paired with multiple short put spreads (keeping total account delta at `-0.07`, Case 145).
- **Put-Call Parity Equivalence with Bearish BWB (Case 160)**:
  - Bearish BWB = Buy upper Put Spread (0.5/0.4) + Sell lower Put Spread (0.15/0.05).
  - Iron Condor = Sell upper Call Spread (0.5/0.4) + Sell lower Put Spread (0.15/0.05).
  - **Why two distinct names**: **Liquidity management (avoiding ITM options)**. At the exact same strikes, buying a `0.5/0.4 Put Spread` is identical to selling a `0.6/0.5 ITM Call Spread`; to avoid wide bid-ask spreads on ITM calls, you buy the `0.5/0.4 Put Spread` (forming the BWB).
- **Iron Condor vs. BWB Profit Profiles (Cases 7, 49, 161)**: Iron Condors yield higher profits during flat consolidation (since the short call is ATM; on 0DTE, P&L diverges noticeably after **3 hours of holding**, Case 7); BWBs peak when price declines to the first short put strike. The disadvantage of the Iron Condor is that during a violent plunge, upper call spread gains are capped while lower put spread losses can far exceed them (Case 161).

### (3) Deep OTM Put Credit Spread (Dip-Buying Mechanism)
- **Leg Structure (1 Short, 1 Long = 2 Legs)**:
  - Sell 1 **Delta 0.15 Put** (or 0.13 / 0.10, deep OTM)
  - Buy 1 **Delta 0.05 Put** (or 0.09 / 0.05)
- **Net Delta per Tranche**: **+0.04 to +0.05** (0.10 delta doubles the risk/reward).
- **Expiration Target**: **1–5 Days** (Xiaoxiao personally prefers 1–3 days in Cases 249, 271, 286, 315; novices should use 5 days / Friday expiry); when VXN spikes, may extend to 1–2 weeks or 1–2 months (Case 188), or match the upper call spread's expiration (Cases 180, 198); when VXN is low, never sell puts beyond 2 weeks.
- **Dip-Buying Thesis**: Following a sharp drop, elevated IV combined with index put skew inflates OTM puts, creating positive expectancy. Even if the market continues to drift down slowly, the spread expires worthless for full profit as long as it is not breached; if breached in a crash, its short DTE allows rescuing with a butterfly (Case 249).
- **Scaling In**: Add one tranche (+0.04 to +0.05 delta) for every 100–200 point NQ drop, **assigning each tranche a distinct expiration date** to diversify single-day settlement risk (Cases 171, 253).
- **Never use this structure to "take assignment"**: Selling puts to take delivery is a disguised high-delta long bet with severe downside risk.

### (4) Covered Call / Cash-Secured Put & The 4% Implied Loan Interest
- **If holding cash**: Buy 100 shares + Sell 1-month ATM Call (strike $\approx$ spot price).
- **If cash-constrained**: Sell 1–2 month ATM naked Put.
- **Put-Call Parity & 4% Implied Interest Rate (Case 268)**: Long 100 shares + Short Call (same strike/expiry) $\equiv$ Short Put. Any bullish option strategy that does not deploy cash (e.g., buying calls) implicitly **pays ~4% annual loan interest** baked into option pricing; buying 100 shares with cash + selling an ATM Covered Call (or selling a Put) **collects/earns that 4% annual interest**.
- Hedging long stock: Selling a 1-month ATM call adds -0.50 delta (cutting 50% exposure). When hedging oversized leveraged ETFs (e.g., $50k of TQQQ = $150k notional exposure), reduce share size directly rather than buying mismatched single-leg puts (Case 70).

### (5) Classic Butterfly (Symmetric Wings) & Put Ratio Spread (1:2)
- **Classic Butterfly**: Equal wing widths mandate negative delta and negative theta; used exclusively as an emergency defense tool to rescue breached short puts (see Section 6).
- **Put Ratio Spread (1:2 — Prohibited)**: Buying 1 delta 0.50 put and selling 2 delta 0.25 puts leaves uncapped downside risk; a 7sd crash inflicts a $25 loss vs. $4–5 on a BWB.

### (6) Naked Put Selling (Treasuries / JPY Exception & The 7sd Lesson)
- Bullish **30-Year Treasury Futures (ZB)** or **Japanese Yen Futures (JPY)**: Sell naked puts, ~1 month DTE, 0.30–0.50 delta (low volatility, strong macro support at 107/108 on ZB).
- **Real-world lesson on missing rallies via naked puts (Cases 301, 309, 311)**: Xiaoxiao sold JPY `0.0063` naked puts hoping to take delivery, but a 3% (7sd) surge in JPY the day before expiration wiped out his put delta to 0, causing him to miss the entire subsequent rally.

### (7) Diagonal Spreads & LEAPS / PMCC (Counterexamples & Trapped LEAPS Rescue)
- Wide-gap diagonals carry severe IV mispricing risk; buying back-month options in high IV is fatal. **High-IV 1-day exception (`末日铁鹰 / 断翅蝴蝶 diagonal`, Case 49)**: Selling 0DTE short legs while buying 1DTE protective long legs (1-day gap).
- **How to Rescue a Trapped Illiquid LEAPS Spread (SNDK Sep 2027 Case Study, Case 271)**: Buying a far-dated deep OTM Call Spread for ~$5,500 yields tiny delta (`0.053 delta` = 5.3 shares) while wide bid-ask spreads (`45.60 / 73.60`) lock in an immediate >$500 (-10%) slippage loss. If trapped, **do NOT liquidate immediately (which locks in a second massive slippage loss)**: either hold for ~1 year until liquidity improves near expiry, or **sell a ~3-month OTM Call Spread with `-0.05 delta` to neutralize total Delta to 0**, insulating net value from stock swings.

## 3. Expiration Selection Logic (Exhaustive)

- **0DTE / Same-Day Expirations**:
  - When VXN / single-day sd is high, 0DTE Bearish BWBs amplify strike decay differentials; if building a negative-delta combo, both 0DTE BWB and IC work (Case 67). When single-day sd is very low, avoid 0DTE BWB (Case 242); active screen-watchers can trade 0DTE ICs (Cases 49, 63).
  - **0DTE Profit-Taking Window & Asian Timezone Auto-Close Order (Cases 1, 5, 9, 14)**: Close 0DTE combos between **1:00–3:00 PM ET** (capturing 50%–75% of max profit); traders in Asian timezones who cannot stay awake can place an automated closing order set for **2:10 PM ET** (Cases 5, 9).
- **Same-Day vs. Next-Day Entry**: Morning entry $\rightarrow$ 0DTE; afternoon/evening entry $\rightarrow$ 1DTE.
- **1–3 Week Horizons**: In high VXN or frequent 3sd whipsaw markets, use 1–3 week `0.5/0.4/0.15/0.05` BWBs or Iron Condors for wide protection and adjustment flexibility (Cases 68–119, 232).
- **Prioritize Friday Expirations** (peak liquidity); avoid newly listed daily cycles; never trade >90 DTE.
- **Nuance on Low IV During Rallies (Cases 212, 237, 241, 243)**: As rallies compress IV, **never extend DTE to *sell* extrinsic value**; however, **low IV is advantageous for *buying* longer-dated extrinsic value** (e.g., when rescuing a breached upper Call Spread by buying a `0.5/0.4 ATM Put Spread`, extending DTE by 4–7 days into next week is actually more favorable, Case 243).
- **Quarterly Futures Expiration & Contango Basis (+200–300 pts, Cases 28–30, 145, 295, 348, 352)**:
  - Expiring quarterly NQ futures and AM options cash-settle at **9:30 AM Friday open** (extreme volatility and trading halts occur in the final 3 minutes; **margin is freed immediately after 9:30 AM**, even though IBKR continues displaying the expired leg until evening batch processing, Case 352). A parallel PM option chain on the next quarter's NQ contract expires at **4:00 PM close**.
  - **Cross-Quarter Futures Contango (+200–300 pts / 4% Annual Interest Carry, Cases 145, 295)**: Because of the 4% annual risk-free interest rate (~1% per quarter), December NQ futures naturally trade **200–300 points higher** than September NQ futures! Always benchmark far-month option strikes against the corresponding far-month futures price; this 1% quarterly downward convergence acts as a structural tailwind for NQ shorts (Case 295).

## 4. Instrument Selection & Execution Infrastructure (Exhaustive)

- **NQ (E-mini Nasdaq-100 Futures Options — Primary Choice)**:
  - Multiplier: $20/pt (~$570k–$600k notional), 8x QQQ, saving >70% in fees.
  - **23-Hour Trading** (Case 158: halts only for 1 hour daily from **5:00–6:00 PM ET**; IG Weekend Nasdaq exists for dealer hedging over weekends but spreads are too wide to trade, Cases 158, 325).
  - **Punctual 4:00 PM ET Cash Settlement**.
  - **IBKR 3:40 PM ET Forced Liquidation (`强平`) Risk (Case 26)**: If an account lacks the ~$50,000 overnight margin required to hold 1 NQ futures contract, **IBKR's risk engine will automatically liquidate expiring NQ options near ATM/ITM at market around 3:40 PM ET** (inflicting severe slippage).
- **QQQ (Nasdaq-100 ETF Options — Secondary Choice)**:

## 6. Adjustment Strategies & Defensive Playbooks (Critical Core)

### (1) Core Defensive Philosophy
- **Three Operational Directions**: Expire worthless, scale into new tranches, or roll out in time. Yet Xiaoxiao also noted the philosophical paradox in Case 155: ***"If you never leave this gambling table, the money you make is never truly yours."***
- **Adjustments inherently carry negative expectancy** (~$20 friction per roll). Adjust only when failing to act leads to max loss or holds a negative-EV leg (such as an OTM short call).
- **Paying a debit to roll $\neq$ losing money; collecting a credit $\neq$ making money**.

### (2) Quantitative Adjustment Triggers & Pre-Expiry (~3 Days Left) Review Rules
- Upper **Short Call (OTM) drops 150–200 points** below spot (or delta drops to **0.40 prepare / 0.35 standard / 0.30 mandatory**) $\rightarrow$ roll down and out.
  - **Why delaying until 650–800 pts OTM causes "Double-Sided Losses (`两头亏`)" (Case 165)**: If you fail to roll every ~200 pts during a drop, your upper call quickly goes deep OTM and under-earns on the way down; if you then roll it down 650 pts at the bottom, a subsequent bounce inflicts heavy losses on the way back up.
- Spot breaches the first short strike by **>100 points** $\rightarrow$ evaluate defense; **>300 points** $\rightarrow$ adjust immediately (Case 123).
- **Pre-Expiry (~3 Days Remaining) Review & Action Rules (Cases 93, 160, 223, 253, 295, 297, 333, 344, 347)**:
  1. **Why Upper OTM Short Call Spreads Must Be Rolled Early (Cases 253, 333, 344)**: When a drop leaves your upper short call OTM (`delta <= 0.35` or `0.25`), index put skew makes **holding OTM short calls a negative-EV trade** where a sudden rally causes a 10x price explosion (Case 344)—roll it out to next week's ATM call spread promptly.
  2. **True Nature of Holding Near-ATM to Expiry & "Running the Horse (`跑马`)" (Cases 295, 347)**:
     - In Case 295, Xiaoxiao stated there is **no mathematical reason to consider holding near-ATM options to expiry negative EV**, though huge delta swings make it unsuitable for novices.
     - In Case 347, when a student asked if one must always roll 3 days before expiry to avoid "turning a free roll into a horse race (9:30 AM cash settlement)," Xiaoxiao replied: ***"You can't entirely say that" (`不能完全这么说`)***—explaining that cash settlement is mathematically fair (`跑马本质上是公平的`), and the real reason he rolled on Tuesday was to **free up negative delta so he could dip-buy on Wednesday's crash**.
  3. **3-Step Pre-Expiry Check for 1–3 Week BWBs (Cases 93, 108, 160, 223)**:
     - Step 1: Is spot between the two short put strikes? **If not, consider closing or rolling ~3 days before expiry to avoid excessive final-day gamma risk** (Case 93).
     - Step 2: If spot is between the short strikes, does the combo's current net delta match today's directional view? **If not, close early to lock in positive theta gains** (Cases 75, 89, 93).
     - Step 3: If both hold, hold to expiration. Additionally, if a rally left the BWB's upper `Long Put Spread` OTM, roll it out a week rather than letting the bought spread decay to zero (Cases 160, 223).
  4. **Lower OTM Put Credit Spreads (Cases 180, 253, 297)**: **If unbreached (OTM), hold to expiration to decay to zero**; if breached, rescue with a butterfly (Case 297).

### (3) Downside Defense: Rolling Upper Bearish Spreads Down & Out
- **Standard Operation (Cases 108, 110, 122, 123, 177, 198)**: Close the legacy upper spread (whether an old short call spread or the breached upper long put spread of a 1–3 week BWB) $\rightarrow$ sell **next week's (or far-month) ATM Call Spread (Sell ATM Call, Buy OTM Call)**, configuring the 4-leg roll itself to **Delta 0** (or `+0.03 to +0.05` delta if dip-buying).
  - If rolling a BWB's upper put spread into next week's put spread: keeping the **same strikes** naturally yields **`+0.026 to +0.05` positive delta** (mild dip-buying); keeping strict **Delta 0** requires **widening** next week's upper put spread (Cases 106–108).
- **6-Leg Consolidation Roll (Case 127)**: Two separate OTM upper spreads can be closed together and rolled into a single far-month ATM Call Spread in one 6-leg Delta-0 order to save fees.

### (4) Severe Downside Defense: Rescuing Breached Short Put Spreads & Second-Breach Leg Cancellation
- **Trigger & Timing**: When spot touches or breaches the lower short put (`delta 0.50 to 0.65`, or breached by ~200 pts on a 1-week option, Cases 143, 173, 249); **execute during an intraday bounce** whenever possible to reduce intrinsic value cost (Case 142).
- **Buying a Butterfly or Broken-Wing Butterfly to Roll Down (Spoken as 6 Legs / Displays as 5 Legs in IBKR, Cases 129, 135, 143, 170, 204, 249)**:
  1. **Buy 1** existing breached short put (closing $K_{upper}$).
  2. **Sell 2** lower puts (delta `0.25–0.35`, center body $K_{body}$).
  3. **Buy 1** lowest protective put ($K_{wing}$).
  4. **Balance Negative Delta (Legs 5 & 6)**: Because an equal-width butterfly carries **negative Delta (and negative Theta)**, simultaneously **sell a next-week deep OTM put credit spread** so the 6-leg (5-strike) adjustment totals **Net Delta 0**.
- **True Rule on Butterfly Wing Widths (Cases 129, 135, 143, 204, 206, 249)**:
  - In Cases 135 & 143, Xiaoxiao explicitly stated you can use **either a symmetric butterfly or a broken-wing butterfly (`只要两侧宽度不要差太多`)** with matching center body strikes (Case 129) and wing widths around 1 sd (**200–400 pts**; e.g., Case 135 used `28900/28500/28200` with 400 vs. 300 pt wings; Case 143 used `28700/28300/27900` with 400 pt equal wings).
  - **Why equal-width ($K_{upper} - K_{body} == K_{body} - K_{wing}$) is preferred (Cases 204, 206, 249)**: An equal-width butterfly has **zero net payoff below the lowest wing at expiration**, meaning it **adds zero additional maximum downside risk beyond your original Put Spread's max loss** while keeping long and short contract counts equal (2 bought, 2 sold) to avoid large Vega/Gamma exposure.
- **[Advanced Core] Reusing Existing Long Puts & Second-Breach Automatic Leg Cancellation (Cases 204, 249, 289, 291)**:
  - **Reusing existing legs on first breach (Cases 289, 291)**: On a narrower spread (e.g., 100–200 pts wide), when the short put is breached, your existing protective `Long Put` is often already at `delta 0.30–0.35`—so **2 of the 4 butterfly legs are your existing short put spread**!
  - **Second Butterfly Rescue (`二次买蝴蝶`) cleans out old legs without accumulating 10 legs (Cases 204, 249)**:
    - Suppose an initial 500-wide `29000/28500` Put Spread is breached at `29000` and rescued with a 300-wide butterfly (`+1*29000P, -2*28700P, +1*28400P`), leaving two narrower spreads: `-2*28700P, +1*28500P, +1*28400P` (widths 200 and 300).
    - If spot crashes further to `28700` (**second breach**), buying a second butterfly places the **2 newly sold center puts directly at `28500` or `28400`, automatically closing out your existing `+1*28500P` and `+1*28400P` legs** and leaving just 2 new lower OTM short puts!
- **Complete 3-Stage 0DTE Breached Put Rescue Sequence (Cases 51, 128–132, 138, 171)**:
  - **Stage 1 (Cases 128, 129)**: Split a `0.60 delta` 0DTE breach into `0.20–0.30` today + `0.30–0.40` Monday, or buy a same-day butterfly using an **ATM Long Call** as the upper wing (`+28800C / -2*28600P / +28400P`).
  - **Stage 2 (Wait 2–3 Hours + Roll Stalled ATM Call, Cases 129, 130, 138)**: **Wait 2–3 hours** for the 0DTE short put to decay; if spot stalls near the 0DTE ATM Long Call, roll that Call **up 100 pts + buy Monday's ATM Call Spread** at Delta 0 so both 0DTE legs expire worthless.
  - **Stage 3 (Post-0DTE Split & Why Far-Month Uses `0.25/0.15`, Cases 131, 132)**: Once the 0DTE put expires, roll Monday's Call Spread into a Friday `0.15/0.05` Put Spread + a far-month **`0.25/0.15` Put Spread** (using `0.15/0.05` on a far-month expiry requires an excessively wide spread with too much crash risk, Case 132).
  - **Final 2 Hours & 4:55 PM Rule (Case 171)**: In the final 2 hours of 0DTE, no extrinsic remains to adjust; if still ITM at **3:55 / 4:55 PM**, stop out and close (having captured maximum final-day theta decay to offset the delta loss), and if still bullish at the lows, open a next-day ATM Put Spread (`~0.20 delta`).
- **Alternative Extreme-Crash Rescue: Buying Upper OTM Call Spreads for a Violent Snapback (Cases 188, 190, 191, 249 — Use with Caution)**:
  - After a multi-day waterfall crash, instead of selling lower OTM puts, **buy an upper 0DTE/short-dated OTM Call Spread (`~+0.20 net delta`)** to bet on a violent short-covering rally; once it bounces to ATM, close the Call Spread and simultaneously sell a 2–5 day deep OTM Put Spread in a 4-leg Delta-0 order (converting into an Iron Condor, Case 190).

### (5) Upside Defense: Managing Bullish Squeezes & Important DTE Correction

- **Rescuing a Deep ITM Short Call Spread & Crucial DTE Correction (Case 212 vs. Cases 237, 241, 243, 280, 308)**:

Thus, when buying the `0.5/0.4 ATM Put Spread` (or rolling the upper spread), **extending DTE by 4–7 days into next week is superior**.

## 8. Option Greeks, Pricing Mechanics & Market Microstructure (Exhaustive)

- **Path-Independence of Option Pricing (`即时性`, Case 5)**: Option value depends solely on current $(S, t, \sigma)$, not the path taken. A 0DTE BWB profits on a 1sd drop by the close via full-day Theta decay—but if spot **drops 1sd within 1 minute of the open**, time $t$ has not elapsed yet, so negative Gamma causes an immediate unrealized loss until Theta decays over the day.
- **Irrelevance of Vega vs. The Vanna Short-Covering Squeeze (Cases 55, 59)**:
  - Ignore Vega in routine trading (chicken-and-egg with realized vol).
  - **Vanna Effect ($\partial \Delta / \partial \sigma$, Cases 55, 59)**: When traders buy puts ahead of a feared catalyst, dealers sell puts and short futures to delta-hedge (DDH). Once the event passes and **IV crushes (Vanna)** or puts are closed, put delta collapses toward zero, forcing dealers to **aggressively buy back short futures** and triggering a vertical index rally even without bullish news.
- **Theta-Gamma Trade-Off & Zero-Gamma Symmetric Spreads**: Positive theta mandates negative gamma; however, a vertical spread straddling spot symmetrically (e.g., `0.60 / 0.40` delta call spread) has **0 Gamma and 0 Theta** at its center point on entry (Case 69).
- **Calculus of IBKR Performance Graphs (Cases 133, 135, 140)**:
  - Set the projection date to **2–4 days ahead** (Case 133).
  - **Why Only Graph 1 $V(S)$ Matters (Case 135)**: Graph 1 is the P&L function $V(S)$, Graph 2 is $\text{Delta} = dV/dS$ (first derivative), and Graph 3 is $\text{Gamma} = d^2V/dS^2$ (second derivative)—since derivatives contain no new information beyond the original function, only Graph 1 is needed.
  - **Proper Back-Testing (Case 140)**: Screenshot Wednesday's projection set to Friday and compare it against actual P&L on Friday—do not dial Friday's chart backward to Wednesday.
- **Dealer DDH & Quadruple Witching "杀期权 (Max Pain Pinning)" Microstructure (Cases 28–30, 89, 93, 325, 348, 349, 352)**:
  - ~80% of equity volume comes from option hedging ("tail wags the dog"). During thin holiday/night sessions, dealer DDH creates air-pocket waterfall crashes or vertical squeezes (Cases 89, 93).
  - **8:00–9:30 AM Quarterly Settlement Pinning ("杀期权", Cases 348, 349)**: Ahead of the 9:30 AM quarterly cash settlement, institutional sellers push futures toward peak Call Open Interest strikes (e.g., NQ 29550 / ES 7650) to wipe out option value, while ITM call holders short expiring futures to lock in gains.
- **Debunking Crypto Perpetual vs. Stock 3% Spread Arbitrage (Case 211)**: Backtesting 15-min K-lines uses stale `Last` prices on illiquid contracts; live bid-ask spreads completely erase the apparent 3% arbitrage gap.
